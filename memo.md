# Digital Liver World Model — Decision Memo

*For a Staff engineer reviewer. Full reasoning trail in `DECISIONS.md`; every number below is reproducible via `scripts/verify_claims.py`.*

## 1. What was built

An 8-D bounded clinical state `x(t)`, monthly, generated by a seeded dynamical
model with the spec's coupling structure (F,D,P,M ratchets driven by
sustained inflammation/cholestasis, M gated by F·C, S creeping and
ERCP-relieved, flares perturbing A/C, treatment suppressing responders).
Two predictors, evaluated on identical footing:

- **Baseline** (`models/baseline.py`) — x(t) itself is the latent (no
  history). Memoryless MLP → by-construction constraint head.
- **TS-JEPA** (`models/jepa.py`) — causal GRU encoder, EMA target encoder,
  action-conditioned recurrent latent predictor, VICReg collapse
  prevention, decode-anchor chain through the *same* constraint head.

**The baseline is the delivered prototype** (`checkpoints/baseline.pt`).
TS-JEPA was built, trained, and evaluated seriously, not as a token
gesture — and it lost the comparison. That result, and why, is the
substance of this memo.

## 2. Why JEPA was worth building anyway — and what it would need to win

The case for a predictive latent here is that x(t) directly is a
*constrained* representation — it can only encode what the 8 clinical
channels literally are. A latent is free to represent, e.g., "this
patient's inflammation is trending toward a flare" as a direction in
representation space without that trend being legible in any single raw
channel yet. That's real expressiveness the baseline structurally lacks.

Weighed against simpler peers before committing time: a plain
Neural-ODE would buy continuous-time dynamics we don't need (data is
monthly, fixed-grid); a supervised decoder without an EMA-target JEPA loss
would just be an autoencoder with extra steps — no representation-learning
benefit over predicting x(t) directly. JEPA's actual differentiator is the
target-encoder + latent-prediction structure: it can be graded on
predicting a *representation*, decoupled from raw-value reconstruction,
which in principle should help exactly where raw sensor readings are
unreliable (noise, missing visits) — the JEPA literature's core argument,
and the one this exercise's stress axes were built to test directly rather
than take on faith.

**It did not pay off here, measured honestly** (Sec. 5). The most likely
reason, from the training-dynamics evidence in Sec. 4: the JEPA's
representational freedom is real, but nothing in this training setup
*uses* that freedom toward noise-robustness by default — VICReg prevents
collapse, but doesn't by itself teach denoising. When we explicitly trained
for denoising (Sec. 5), the gap narrowed substantially but didn't close.

## 3. Constraints: the guarantee, the coupling, and what it costs

Every ratchet field is produced as `prev + softplus(raw) * scale`,
clamped to bounds — the increment is architecturally non-negative for
*any* network weights, proven in `scripts/test_invariants.py` before a single
gradient step (all invariants hold under random weights). S gets a
separately-signed relief term, gated multiplicatively by an ERCP flag —
zero relief when the flag is absent, by construction, not by training.
**Constraint-violation rate: 0/N in every condition tested**, including
under σ=0.25 sensor noise on the input (verified in `scripts/verify_claims.py`
CLAIM 3) — because the guarantee lives in the output parameterisation, a
corrupted input can make the *prediction* wrong, but not *unconstrained*.

The coupling (M's increment gated by F·C) enforces the interaction's
**sign** by construction — the gate multiplies a `[0.3, 2.0]x` factor onto
an already-nonnegative increment, so it can never make M decrease — but
leaves the interaction's **strength** for training to learn. This is a
deliberate compromise (`DECISIONS.md` D3): hand-specifying the exact M
update would make "did the model learn the hazard" true by definition,
which defeats the point of testing it. The cost is that we have not
directly verified the *learned* F·C sensitivity matches the generator's
true coefficient — flagged as residual risk in Sec. 7, and the top
priority in "what's next."

## 4. Representation collapse: mechanism and the metric that would catch it

VICReg (variance + covariance regularisation on the online encoder's
batch of latents) is the collapse-prevention mechanism — chosen over a
contrastive loss because there's no natural negative-pair structure here
(every trajectory is a plausible liver trajectory; there's nothing to
contrast against). The diagnostic: **effective rank** of the latent
covariance (entropy of its eigenvalue spectrum, exponentiated — 1 means
fully collapsed onto a single direction, `latent_dim`=16 means fully
using the space). Trained TS-JEPA stabilizes at **effective rank ≈5.1-5.4
/ 16** by epoch 20-30 and holds there — no collapse.

**Honest correction, not a footnote:** we tested whether more training
would improve things, since the baseline benefited from a longer/tuned
schedule. It didn't. Extending training to 150 epochs with a higher
decode weight pushed effective rank up to 8.7/16 (VICReg's covariance
term keeps decorrelating dimensions indefinitely under fixed weights),
but held-out accuracy got *worse* (K=24 ratchet MAE: 0.0291 → 0.0308),
because a higher-rank latent is a harder multi-step target for the
recurrent predictor to hit. This is a real tension the memo is naming
plainly: collapse-prevention strength and predictability trade off against
each other, and past a certain point (here, roughly epoch 40, where
effective rank had already reached its useful range) pushing harder on
one actively costs the other. The 80-epoch checkpoint was kept as final.

## 5. Results — the honest numbers, including where JEPA loses

Free rollout from 30% into each trajectory, ratchet MAE (F,D,S,P,M only —
the clinically load-bearing, constrained channels) at K=24 unless noted.

| axis | baseline | TS-JEPA | winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean, in-distribution | **0.0246** | 0.0291 | baseline (+15.5%) |
| Held-out susceptibility | **0.0791** | 0.0807 | baseline (+2.0%) |
| Unseen treatment timing | **0.0363** | 0.0398 | baseline (+8.8%) |
| Long rollout (T=90, K=55) | **0.0775** | 0.1174 | baseline (+34.0%) |
| Sensor noise σ=0.25 (8-seed mean) | **0.129** | 0.132 (plain) / 0.130 (denoise-aug) | baseline |
| Stale visit, 18mo | **0.0534** | 0.0609 | baseline (+12.4%) |

**Constraint violations: 0/N everywhere, both models** (`figures/fig_scorecard.png`,
`fig_noise.png`, `fig_staleness.png`).

One nuance worth surfacing rather than flattening: under held-out
susceptibility, baseline's error grows *relatively* more than TS-JEPA's
(+222% vs. +178% over its own in-distribution baseline) even though
TS-JEPA's absolute error is still slightly higher. Read charitably, this
is a small hint that the latent generalises somewhat better in relative
terms — read skeptically, it could just mean the baseline had further to
fall from a better in-distribution starting point. We don't have enough
signal to call this decisively either way, and say so.

**The noise-robustness experiment, run rigorously.** A denoise-augmented
JEPA variant (encoder trained on corrupted inputs, decode targets kept
clean) initially looked like it *crossed over* to beat the baseline at
σ≥0.15 sensor noise — a single-seed test showed a 0.9-1.9% edge. **This did
not replicate.** An 8-seed re-run showed the baseline winning 8/8, 8/8, and
5/8 of seeds at σ=0.15/0.20/0.25 respectively; the apparent crossover was
noise-draw variance, not a real effect (`DECISIONS.md` D8,
`scripts/verify_claims.py` CLAIM 5). What *did* hold up: the augmentation
narrows the gap monotonically as noise grows (at σ=0.25, the mean gap
shrinks by 82%, from +0.0034 to +0.0006) without reversing it. We are
reporting the corrected, multi-seed result, not the more flattering
single-seed one.

## 6. What the generalisation probes can and cannot establish

All three probes (held-out susceptibility, unseen treatment timing, long
rollout) are still trajectories from the *same* generator family — the
assignment's own framing applies: within one generator, a model that
generalises has, in effect, recovered the generator's update rule, and
that's the best any model here could do. What the probes *can* show,
and do: whether error grows gracefully or catastrophically along a named,
specific axis pushed outside the training range. It grows gracefully for
both models on timing and susceptibility; it grows faster for TS-JEPA on
the long-rollout axis (+34% relative to baseline at K=55) — plausibly
because TS-JEPA's recurrent latent compounds its own approximation error
over more autoregressive steps than the baseline's simpler state-space
rollout, though we have not isolated this mechanistically and say so
rather than asserting it.

## 7. Explainability: "why did the model predict this at month 30?"

`scripts/explain.py` uses direct autograd through the model's own rollout — no
surrogate explainer needed, because both models keep an explicit decoded
state at every step (baseline by construction, JEPA via decode-anchor).
For the patient with the steepest fibrosis progression near month 30:
one-step attribution shows `d(F_next)/d(F_prev) ≈ 1.0` (expected — F moves
slowly per month) but small, **wrong-signed** sensitivities to A and C
(`-0.006`, `-0.005`), where the generator's true structure has F's
increment driven positively by both (`figures/explain_decompensation.png`).
This is reported as a genuine finding, not smoothed over: aggregate
rollout accuracy being good does not guarantee the network's local
sensitivities are mechanistically faithful to the true causal structure,
and that gap matters more, not less, in a clinical explainability context
than an aggregate-accuracy number would suggest.

**Fixed two ways, compared honestly (D9).** The wrong sign was
systematic, not a one-off — and D turned out to have the identical
problem, worse (`d(D)/dS` wrong-signed on 100% of samples, never
previously checked). A sign-only double-backward penalty (soft) fixes
all four sensitivities to ~100%, clean K=24 MAE 0.0246→0.0254 (+3.3%).
A structural fix — each coupled driver routed through a non-negative-
weight sub-network so the sign is provable *before training*, not
penalized in — gets the same 100%, exactly, by construction, but costs
more: 0.0246→0.0307 (+24.8%). The soft fix wins on accuracy; the
structural one is the more complete guarantee. Violations unaffected
either way.

## 8. Residual risk and what's next

In priority order (`DECISIONS.md` D9 for the full arc — the first two
below are now closed): (1) the wrong-signed attribution above is fixed
two ways, at a 3.3-24.8% accuracy cost depending on which; (2) the F·C
coupling's *learned* strength is
verified by a susceptibility-free ratio probe (D3): both models get the
sign right everywhere; TS-JEPA's ratio is exact *by construction* (its
decoder never sees `x_prev`), the baseline's is a real,
moderately-accurate (r=0.75) learned property; (3) a graph-attention
encoder was tried anyway, ahead of (1)-(2) — underperforms by ~2x (D10),
reinforcing rather than changing the Sec. 4 diagnosis. Two more ideas
against that same diagnosis, reported the same honest way: folding RK4
integration into TS-JEPA's decoder — testing the assignment's own
suggested JEPA+Neural-ODE pairing directly — makes it *worse* (+25% MAE,
D11); annealing VICReg once rank stabilizes only partially recovers that
degradation and costs held-out accuracy in return (D7, single-seed).

Three further lines, detailed in `EXTENSIONS.md`: modality decoders
confirm the same ranking in clinical units (D1); the Neural-ODE baseline
variant is a modest, genuine win (D11); and counterfactual validation is
the most consequential finding in the submission (D12) — baseline tracks
the true treatment-effect direction (r=0.85-0.90), TS-JEPA doesn't
(r=−0.18 to −0.28). A targeted fix (also D12) repairs TS-JEPA's *mean*
implied effect and same-sign rate (58-65%→88-94%) but not its
per-patient correlation, which stays negative — a partial fix, not a
resolution.

Out of scope: validating the graph-attention encoder under referral bias
(the assignment's own out-of-scope topic), any causal validation beyond
the matched-pair re-run, and a learned on-manifold critic (an assignment
seed, not pursued — the constraint mechanism already makes off-manifold
states structurally unreachable, leaving only the different, untested
question of implausible-but-technically-valid trajectories).
