01 The brief

Accurate, constrained, explainable — pick your tension

One organizing construct carries three jobs at once, and the exercise is built so those jobs pull against each other.

A single, low-dimensional, bounded clinical state vector x(t) is the thing a generator emits data from, the decode target of a learned world model, and an auditable record of what the model believes about this patient right now.
Accurate

Predict the disease

Given x(t0…tk), roll forward to x̂(tk+1…tn) — and do it on trajectories the generator can still surprise you with.

Constrained

Never reverse the irreversible

F, D, P, M only go up; S only goes down at an ERCP. A trajectory that violates this describes a liver that cannot exist.

Explainable

Answer "why?"

A reviewer asking why the model predicted decompensation at month 30 needs a real, mechanistic answer — not a shrug.

The recommended route

A JEPA-style predictive model — one that predicts the representation of a future state, not its raw values — paired naturally with a graph-attention encoder and a Neural-ODE. Weighed seriously against simpler peers (x(t) as the latent, a plain Neural-ODE, a supervised decoder) before committing time to it.

The trap, stated honestly

Within a single generator, a model that generalises has, in effect, recovered the generator's update rule — because here, the generator is the disease. The sharpest thing available is a generalisation probe, not a claim of having learned biology.

04 models/constraints.py

A guarantee that lives in the parameterisation, not the loss

Both models route every raw output through the same ConstraintHead — so a decrease in F, D, P, or M isn't discouraged, it's unrepresentable, for any network weights.

Hard, by construction

Sign is guaranteed

F, D, P: x_prev + softplus(raw)·scale, clamped — softplus is ≥0 for any weights, proven under random init before a single gradient step.

S: same creep term, plus a separately-signed relief multiplicatively gated by the ERCP flag — zero relief when the flag is absent, always.

M: increment gated by prev F·C into a [0.3, 2.0]× multiplier on an already-nonnegative increment — can never make M decrease.

Soft, left to training

Strength is learned

Hand-specifying the exact F·C→M update would make "did the model learn the hazard" true by definition — so only the sign is hard; the strength is a real, testable, learned property.

Verified later with a susceptibility-free ratio probe: both models get the sign right 100% of the time. Baseline's learned ratio correlates r = 0.75 with the true C/F ratio; TS-JEPA's decoder is architecturally pinned to 1.000 — its decoder never sees x_prev at all.

0 / N

Constraint-violation rate in every condition tested, including under σ = 0.25 sensor noise on the input — because the guarantee lives in the output parameterisation, corrupted input can make a prediction wrong, but never unconstrained.

The online encoder is graded on predicting z_target — a representation of the true future produced by an EMA copy of itself — not on reconstructing raw values. The decoder chains its own previous output back in, so the rollout is an honest free rollout, on the same constraint manifold as the baseline, at every step.

Collapse check: effective rank

VICReg holds trained TS-JEPA at effective rank ≈5.1–5.4 / 16 by epoch 20–30 — no collapse. Pushing to 150 epochs raised rank to 8.7/16 but made held-out accuracy worse (0.0291→0.0308): collapse-resistance and predictability trade off past ~epoch 40.

A nuance, not flattened

Under held-out susceptibility, baseline's error grows relatively more than TS-JEPA's (+222% vs +178% over its own in-distribution number) — even though TS-JEPA's absolute error stays slightly higher. Read charitably: the latent generalises somewhat better in relative terms. Read skeptically: the baseline just had further to fall. Not enough signal to call it either way.

07 Explainability

Good aggregate accuracy hid a wrong-signed gradient

scripts/explain.py uses plain autograd through the model's own rollout — no surrogate needed, since both models keep an explicit decoded state every step.

The finding

For the steepest-fibrosis patient near month 30, d(F)/d(F_prev) ≈ 1.0 as expected — but the sensitivities to A and C came back wrong-signed. Checked across 500 samples: d(F)/dA positive only 13.8% of the time, d(F)/dC only 7.4%. D turned out worse — d(D)/dS and d(D)/dA, never previously checked, wrong-signed on 100% of samples.

Fix 1 · soft penalty

Double-backward Jacobian term

relu(−dF/dA) + relu(−dF/dC) + relu(−dD/dS) + relu(−dD/dA), weight 1.0. Result: 100/100/99.3/100% sign-correct. Cost: clean MAE 0.0246→0.0254 (+3.3%).

Fix 2 · structural

Non-negative-weight sub-network

Each coupled driver routed through a MonotonicCoupling block — provably non-decreasing before training, no penalty needed. Result: 100%, exactly, by construction. Cost: 0.0246→0.0307 (+24.8%).

The soft penalty wins on accuracy; the structural fix is the more complete guarantee — and it's what surfaced D's problem in the first place. A hard guarantee bought a real accuracy premium here, not a free lunch.

08 Verdict

Ship the baseline. Here's the residual risk.

TS-JEPA was built, trained, and evaluated seriously — not a token gesture — and it lost the comparison, on accuracy, every generalisation probe, noise robustness, and now causal-direction fidelity too.
